4. Must-haves (all required)
Data model: all four day folders loaded into a model you design and reconciled with the transcripts. When sources conflict, record which one you believed and why.
14 people as AI agents: each one grounded in the data, with a role, workload, history and a personal life. Agents must stay consistent with the records, and anything you invent must be logged as an assumption.
Cockpit: the CEO asks one question and gets a short answer that:
cites the specific records behind it
states a confidence level
says "I don't know" or "the sources disagree" when that's the honest answer
"What should I worry about" view: a ranked list, with each item backed by evidence.
Point-in-time answers: "what did we know on date X?", using the release dates above. Later material must not leak into earlier answers.
Test set: at least 25 automated test questions with expected answers, run with one command. At least 5 must test whether the cockpit refuses or admits uncertainty correctly.
Voice notes: a written response to each CEO voice note (T6a–T6d), committed by 11:59 PM on its release day. Either make the change or push back with your reasons. Agreeing to everything is not a good sign.
Stretch: agents that keep generating plausible new activity after 31 Oct 2026, consistent with the data.

5. AI use: allowed and expected
You may use any AI assistants, models, agents or coding tools you like. The conditions:

You own everything AI produces for you. "The AI wrote it" is not a defence for a wrong answer or a line you can't explain.
Keep an AI_LOG.md: the main tasks you gave AI, where it was wrong, and what you changed. We value honest entries about AI mistakes.
If you're shortlisted, you'll be asked in the interview to explain and change your code without AI help. Build only what you can defend.
AI must not decide which source is true for you. Every conflict you resolve needs your own reasoning in TRANSCRIPT_NOTES.md.
